# FailProducts — Moderation and Community Rules

## 1. Goal

The community should be honest enough to provide useful criticism and safe enough that founders are willing to share failures voluntarily.

Core phrase:

> **Roast the product. Help the builder.**

## 2. Allowed product listings

A listing may represent:

- a live product with little traction;
- an abandoned product;
- a shut-down product;
- a product that failed to monetize;
- a product that failed to attract users;
- a product creator believes failed and wants feedback on.

The submission must describe why the product is being listed.

## 3. Prohibited listings

Reject/remove:

- malware;
- phishing;
- scams;
- products whose listing is intended to distribute illegal content;
- clearly deceptive listings;
- impersonation;
- doxxing;
- personal information dumps;
- content targeting a person rather than a product;
- fabricated accusations presented as facts.

## 4. Community comments

Allowed:

- criticism of the product;
- criticism of positioning, pricing, onboarding, UX, distribution, or strategy;
- suggestions;
- comparisons;
- personal experience with the product;
- constructive disagreement.

Not allowed:

- harassment;
- threats;
- hate/abuse directed at protected groups;
- publishing private information;
- spam;
- repeated self-promotion;
- coordinated attacks;
- claims of criminal/illegal behavior without reliable public evidence;
- targeted insults about a person’s intelligence, appearance, identity, or private life.

## 5. Reporting

Every public product and comment should have a report action.

Report reasons:

- spam;
- harassment;
- impersonation;
- privacy issue;
- scam/malware;
- copyright/trademark concern;
- incorrect information;
- other.

## 6. Moderation states

For products:

- pending;
- published;
- flagged;
- hidden;
- removed.

For comments:

- visible;
- pending;
- hidden;
- removed.

Keep moderation history.

## 7. Founder controls

Creators should be able to:

- edit product information;
- correct false data about their product;
- respond to comments;
- mark the product as recovered;
- request removal;
- report abusive comments.

Creators should not be able to silently delete legitimate community discussion solely because it is negative.

## 8. Accuracy policy

Every public page should distinguish:

**Claimed by creator**

**Community opinion**

**Verified by FailProducts**

Never label an unverified community comment as a factual product diagnosis.

## 9. Automated moderation

MVP should use simple rules first:

- rate limits;
- Turnstile;
- URL validation;
- duplicate detection;
- basic blocked-word/risk filters;
- user report queue.

Do not introduce AI moderation as a hard dependency in MVP.

## 10. Appeals

Provide a simple removal/appeal contact path.

A creator should be able to request:

- correction;
- verification;
- removal for legitimate reason;
- privacy intervention.

## 11. Privacy

Do not publish:

- private email addresses;
- home addresses;
- personal phone numbers;
- private analytics credentials;
- payment credentials;
- secret API tokens.

## 12. Traffic/comeback announcements

Playful announcements must be based on actual observable data.

Good:

> “FailProducts sent 2,400 outbound visits to this product in the last 7 days.”

Not acceptable:

> “This product now has 2,400 total visitors.”

unless verified by a connected analytics source.
